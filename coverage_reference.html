ASD and Alzheimer’s Disease / ADRD Risk
Study Design Reference

This page supports analytic design discussions for a funded NIH study on Alzheimer’s disease and ADRD risk in adults with autism spectrum disorder. The team is working through decisions about which data sources to use, how to define the study cohort, and which comparison group offers the best methodological fit. This reference summarizes those options, with supporting evidence from the literature, in one interactive place.

It is intended for the analytic team and collaborators who are actively shaping the Medicare study design. Content reflects decisions and open questions as of late October 2026 and will be updated as the team aligns.

This is a working draft. Please flag anything that does not match the team’s current thinking.

Dynamic Cohort Design Rationale

Key decisions from the October 28, 2026 call, with supporting evidence from the literature.

No minimum enrollment requirement

ASD adults cycle between Medicaid and Medicare as their waiver and disability status changes. A 12-month continuous enrollment filter would exclude an estimated 50 to 60 percent of the ASD cohort. Under the dynamic open-cohort design, person-time accrues only in enrolled months; enrollment gaps suspend but do not terminate follow-up.

Open cohort with dynamic entry

Beneficiaries enter the ASD cohort when their second qualifying F84.x claim appears (30+ days after the first), following the Burke 2014 and Vivanti 2025 ascertainment standard.[1][2] Entry is permitted in any calendar year from 2008 onward, not only at a fixed baseline date, maximizing sample size across ICD-9 to ICD-10 transition years.

Dual enrollment and SES

Dual-enrolled ASD adults are not necessarily low-income. Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers carry higher or no income limits, so Medicaid eligibility via an HCBS developmental disability waiver does not imply poverty. The DUAL_ELGBL_MONS flag in MBSF identifies this population but does not bring Medicaid claims into the FFS environment.

Dementia ascertainment

Two validated claims-based approaches are available and closely related. Vivanti et al. 2025[2] used the Bynum algorithm (McCarthy / Grodstein 2022)[3][4]: ICD-10 codes F01.xx, F02.xx, F03.xx, G30.xx in Part A/B claims plus Part D dementia medications. Tobi's DREAM study (Desai et al. 2025)[5] uses diagnosis codes only — 1 inpatient or 2 outpatient claims — with no Part D component. Both require a clean washout at cohort entry. The team protocol will specify which standard to apply; the choice should be aligned across the group, particularly with Tobi.

Socioeconomic Status Matching

SES is a major confounder in ADRD research: lower-SES individuals have higher comorbidity burden, worse care access, and different medication use patterns. Because ASD diagnosis and coverage pathways also differ substantially by SES (a late-diagnosed adult on commercial insurance until 65 looks very different from a Medicaid-enrolled adult since childhood), whichever comparison group is selected will require SES adjustment or matching. In Medicare and Medicaid claims data, SES is typically proxied by dual-enrollment status, area deprivation index (ADI) tied to zip code or census tract, or low-income subsidy (LIS) receipt under Part D. The cards below do not score on SES matchability directly, but it should be treated as a constraint that applies across all options.

Citations backing the analytical choices in this document, organized by topic. Numbered references are linked from inline [N] superscripts throughout the page. All PMIDs and DOIs can be verified in PubMed.

1Burke JP, Jain A, Yang W, Kelly JP, Kaiser M, Becker L, Lawer L, Newschaffer CJ. Does a claims diagnosis of autism mean a true case? Autism. 2014;18(3):321–330. doi:10.1177/1362361312467709
Establishes the PPV standard for 2+ claims with ASD diagnosis codes at least 30 days apart. Foundational criterion for the study cohort entry definition.
2Vivanti G, Lee W, Ventimiglia J, Tao S, Lyall K, Shea LL. Prevalence of Dementia Among US Adults With Autism Spectrum Disorder. JAMA Netw Open. 2025;8(1):e2453691. PMID: 39745705. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.53691
Applied the 2-claim F84.x ascertainment standard to 114,582 dual-enrolled Medicare-Medicaid beneficiaries (2014–2016). Reported dementia prevalence 2.56× higher in ASD adults vs. matched controls; used Bynum dementia algorithm.

Medicaid Analytic eXtract 2008–2012 (n = 12,648 ASD-only adults). ASD-only HR 1.96 (95% CI 1.69–2.28) for dementia vs. general Medicaid population; mean age at dementia diagnosis ~49 years. Primary precedent for the autism and dementia risk hypothesis.
