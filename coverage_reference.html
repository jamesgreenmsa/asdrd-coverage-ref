About this reference

This page supports two connected research programs in autism, cognitive aging, and dementia risk. The NIH ASD/ADRD program examines dementia incidence and risk in Medicare-enrolled adults with autism spectrum disorder. The Thome GLP-1 program tests whether GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation is associated with lower ADRD risk, and through which biological pathways, with an ASD subgroup analysis as one aim.

The Shared Evidence Library connects published studies to the specific design questions each program is resolving: which comparators are valid, which outcomes are well-ascertained, where the randomized evidence is null and why, and which analytic choices the published literature supports or challenges.

Working draft. Please flag anything that does not match the team’s current thinking. The coverage timeline, design approach, and comparison group material below reflect the NIH ASD/ADRD program. GLP-1 program content is captured in the Shared Evidence Library and will expand in future updates.

Dynamic Cohort Design Rationale

Key decisions from the October 28, 2026 call, with supporting evidence from the literature.

No minimum enrollment requirement

ASD adults cycle between Medicaid and Medicare as their waiver and disability status changes. A 12-month continuous enrollment filter would exclude an estimated 50 to 60 percent of the ASD cohort (provisional estimate; to be confirmed in the study data). Under the dynamic open-cohort design, person-time accrues only in enrolled months; enrollment gaps suspend but do not terminate follow-up.

Open cohort with dynamic entry

Beneficiaries enter the ASD cohort when their second qualifying F84.x claim appears, following the Burke 2014 and Vivanti 2025 two-claim ascertainment approach.[1][2] A minimum 30-day separation between qualifying claims is a proposed operational requirement pending team confirmation of the specific interval. Entry is permitted in any calendar year from 2008 onward, not only at a fixed baseline date. Pre-October 2015 entry requires ICD-9-CM code 299.x; October 2015 onward uses ICD-10-CM F84.x. A stated rule is needed for linking qualifying claims across the coding transition (e.g., whether a 299.x claim and an F84.x claim within the same observation window count as the two-claim pair).

Dual enrollment and SES

Dual-enrolled ASD adults are not necessarily low-income. Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers carry higher or no income limits, so Medicaid eligibility via an HCBS developmental disability waiver does not imply poverty. The DUAL_ELGBL_MONS flag in MBSF identifies this population but does not bring Medicaid claims into the FFS environment.

Dementia ascertainment

Two validated claims-based approaches are available and closely related. Vivanti et al. 2025[2] used the Bynum-standard algorithm, described and validated by McCarthy et al. (2022) and Grodstein et al. (2022)[3][4]: qualifying dementia diagnosis codes in Medicare FFS Parts A/B claims only — either ≥1 inpatient/SNF/home health/hospice claim, or ≥2 carrier or outpatient claims at least 7 days apart. Part D medication claims are not included. Tobi's DREAM study (Desai et al. 2025)[5] uses diagnosis codes in 1 inpatient or 2 outpatient claims with no Part D component — structurally similar to Bynum; exact qualifying code lists and observation windows will need to be reconciled. The team protocol will specify which standard to apply; the choice should be aligned across the group, particularly with Tobi.

Socioeconomic Status Matching

SES is a major confounder in ADRD research: lower-SES individuals have higher comorbidity burden, worse care access, and different medication use patterns. Because ASD diagnosis and coverage pathways also differ substantially by SES (a late-diagnosed adult on commercial insurance until 65 looks very different from a Medicaid-enrolled adult since childhood), whichever comparison group is selected will require SES adjustment or matching. In Medicare and Medicaid claims data, SES is typically proxied by dual-enrollment status, area deprivation index (ADI) tied to zip code or census tract, or low-income subsidy (LIS) receipt under Part D. SES matchability is scored explicitly as one of the five dimensions on the comparison cards below. The score reflects how readily available SES proxies (dual-enrollment status, ADI, LIS receipt) can be used to match or adjust for SES differences between the ASD cohort and each candidate comparison group.

Key References

Citations backing the analytical choices in this document, organized by topic. Numbered references are linked from inline [N] superscripts throughout the page. All PMIDs and DOIs can be verified in PubMed.

ASD Ascertainment
1Burke JP, Jain A, Yang W, Kelly JP, Kaiser M, Becker L, Lawer L, Newschaffer CJ. Does a claims diagnosis of autism mean a true case? Autism. 2014;18(3):321–330. doi:10.1177/1362361312467709
Establishes the PPV standard for 2+ ASD diagnosis code claims on separate dates (PPV 87.4% in a pediatric Medicaid validation sample). Supports the two-claim ascertainment approach; a 30-day minimum separation between claims is a proposed operational requirement — the specific interval should be confirmed against a published source before protocol finalization.
2Vivanti G, Lee W, Ventimiglia J, Tao S, Lyall K, Shea LL. Prevalence of Dementia Among US Adults With Autism Spectrum Disorder. JAMA Netw Open. 2025;8(1):e2453691. PMID: 39745705. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.53691
Analyzed 114,582 autistic adults aged 30+ using linked Medicare–Medicaid data (2014–2016). Three-year dementia prevalence: 8.03% (ASD without ID) and 8.88% (ASD with ID). Used the Bynum-standard dementia ascertainment algorithm. Did not include a non-ASD comparison group — cannot independently estimate ASD vs. non-ASD dementia risk. Provides large-scale ASD ascertainment and outcome precedent for Medicare–Medicaid dual-enrolled populations.
Dementia Ascertainment
3Grodstein F, Chang C-H, Capuano AW, Power MC, Marquez DX, Barnes LL, Bennett DA, James BD, Bynum JPW. Identification of Dementia in Recent Medicare Claims Data, Compared With Rigorous Clinical Assessments. J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med Sci. 2022;77(6):1272–1278. PMID: 34919685. PMCID: PMC9159666. doi:10.1093/gerona/glab377
Evaluates Bynum-standard ADRD identification (qualifying dementia diagnosis codes in Medicare Parts A/B FFS claims; Part D medication claims are not included) against rigorous clinical assessments using predominantly ICD-10-era claims. With McCarthy et al. 2022, constitutes the primary validation basis for Bynum-standard outcome ascertainment in this study.
4McCarthy EP, Chang CH, Tilton N, Kabeto MU, Langa KM, Bynum JPW. Validation of Claims Algorithms to Identify Alzheimer's Disease and Related Dementias. J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med Sci. 2022;77(6):1261–1271. PMID: 34919686. PMCID: PMC9159657. doi:10.1093/gerona/glab373
Companion validation paper; together with Grodstein et al. 2022 above, defines the claims algorithm applied by Vivanti et al. 2025 and proposed for this study.
5Desai RJ, Varma VR, Mahesri M, Lee SB, Freedman A, Gerhard T, Segal J, Vine S, Ritchey MBE, Horton DB, Thambisetty M. Population-Based Validation Results From the Drug Repurposing for Effective Alzheimer's Medicines (DREAM) Study. Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics. 2025;117(4):1039–1050 doi:10.1002/cpt.3583
Tobi Gerhard's DREAM study — a related dementia ascertainment approach: ICD-10 diagnosis codes in 1 inpatient or 2 outpatient claims, washout = full baseline period, no Part D drug component. Used in Medicare + CPRD. The team should confirm whether the DREAM or Bynum standard will be applied.
ASD–Dementia Epidemiology
6Vivanti G, Tao S, Lyall K, Robins DL, Shea LL. The prevalence and incidence of early-onset dementia among adults with Autism Spectrum Disorder. Autism Research. 2021;14(10):2189–2199. doi:10.1002/aur.2590
Medicaid Analytic eXtract 2008–2012 (n = 12,648 ASD adults). Adjusted HR for incident dementia vs. general Medicaid population: ASD without ID 1.96 (95% CI 1.69–2.28); combined ASD 2.62 (95% CI 2.40–2.85). Mean age at dementia diagnosis ~49 years. Source of the widely cited ~2.6× hazard ratio estimate. Primary precedent for an ASD–dementia risk association.
7Chang Z, Yao H, Sun S, et al. Association between autism and dementia across generations: evidence from a family study of the Swedish population. Molecular Psychiatry. 2025. PMC12436158. doi:10.1038/s41380-025-03045-6
Swedish national registry data (Sven's arm). Population-based family study documenting the autism–dementia association across generations. Provides international validity context for the US Medicare findings.
US Medicare Enrollment
8Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). Medicare Beneficiaries at a Glance, Data Year 2021. CMS; 2021.
63.9M total Medicare beneficiaries; ~57% in FFS (~36.4M); ~13% under age 65 (~8.3M disability-enrolled). Basis for comparison group pool size estimates used in this document.
9Nikahd M, Hyer M, Wolf B, et al. Risk of Dementia and Related Neurocognitive Disorders Among Autistic and Non-Autistic Older Adults. Age Ageing. 2026;55(2):afag015. doi:10.1093/ageing/afag015
Medicare FFS 2013–2021; 9,201 autistic adults aged 65+ matched 1:2 to 18,356 non-autistic controls. Adjusted subhazard ratio for neurocognitive disorders: 1.20 overall; 1.46 (95% CI 1.36–1.57) for ASD with intellectual disability. Demonstrates that a matched Medicare age-enrolled comparison yields valid dementia risk estimates for older autistic adults and provides published precedent for the age-65+ non-ASD comparator design.
GLP-1 Receptor Agonists and Dementia
10Cummings JL, Atri A, Sano M, et al. EVOKE and EVOKE+: Oral semaglutide in early-stage symptomatic Alzheimer's disease (phase 3 randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials). Lancet. 2026 Mar 19. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)00459-9
Phase 3 RCT of oral semaglutide vs. placebo in amyloid-confirmed early-stage symptomatic AD. Did not slow cognitive decline on CDR-SB. Motivates the prevention-window hypothesis: GLP-1 benefit, if real, may require intervention before clinical memory symptoms are established.
11Tang H, Lu Y, Chen Y, et al. Mediation analysis of GLP-1 receptor agonist use and risk of Alzheimer's disease and related dementias. Alzheimers Dement. 2025. doi:10.1002/alz.70161
T2D new-user cohort (n = 22,908; GLP-1 RA vs DPP-4i; Medicare FFS). Natural indirect effects through body weight and HbA1c ≈1.02 — metabolic pathways explain minimal share of the GLP-1/ADRD association. Precedent for the Thome mediation aim; motivates non-metabolic pathway investigation.
12Wu C, Bhatt MM, Chen Y, et al. GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation and dementia risk: intention-to-treat versus on-treatment analysis. Published 2025.
Active comparator new-user cohort; GLP-1 RA vs DPP-4i. ITT HR 0.95; during-continuous-treatment HR 0.79. Treatment strategy materially changes the effect estimate. Pre-specification of the follow-up estimand is a key design decision for the Thome and James Medicare analyses.
13Zhou Y, et al. Comparative effectiveness of GLP-1 receptor agonists, DPP-4 inhibitors, and SGLT2 inhibitors on dementia risk. Diabetes Obes Metab. 2026. doi:10.1111/dom.70384
Three-arm active comparator study. GLP-1 vs DPP-4i: HR 0.76; GLP-1 vs SGLT2i: HR 1.53. Comparator choice fundamentally changes the apparent treatment effect. Supports DPP-4i as the pre-specified active comparator for the Thome and James Medicare analyses.
14Schechter G, et al. GLP-1 receptor agonists and incident dementia in adults with type 2 diabetes and baseline mild cognitive impairment. Alzheimers Dement Transl Res Clin Interv. 2026. doi:10.1002/trc2.70315
Active comparator cohort in T2D + pre-existing MCI; GLP-1 RA vs DPP-4i. HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.57–0.95). Important for Thome Aim 1 interaction hypothesis. However: this study examines MCI-to-dementia transition, not incident MCI in cognitively unimpaired adults — a distinct clinical question from the HR 0.983 in the James Medicare preliminary data.
15Siqueira JS, et al. GLP-1 receptor agonists and cognitive outcomes in Alzheimer's disease and mild cognitive impairment: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Published October 2026.
Meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (n = 4,132; symptomatic AD or MCI). Pooled CDR-SB effect: 0.09 (95% CI −0.10 to 0.28) — not statistically significant. Together with EVOKE [10], confirms null RCT evidence for treating established symptomatic disease and strengthens the prevention-window hypothesis motivating the Thome observational program.
